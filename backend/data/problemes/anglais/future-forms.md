### Problème 1 : Complete a dialogue about future plans
\begin{enonce}
Two students, Yassine and Salma, are talking about their plans for after the Baccalaureate. Complete the dialogue with the most natural future form of the verb in brackets (will, be going to, present continuous, or present simple).

*"Yassine: So, what ___ (1: you / do) after the exams?*
*Salma: I ___ (2: study) medicine — I decided that a long time ago. And you?*
*Yassine: I'm not sure yet. Maybe I ___ (3: take) a gap year, I think.*
*Salma: That sounds nice. Actually, I ___ (4: meet) my cousin tomorrow to talk about the university forms; she's already a student there.*
*Yassine: Good idea. By the way, the results ___ (5: come out) on the tenth of July, don't they?*
*Salma: Yes. By that day, I ___ (6: send) all my applications already."*
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **are you going to do** (an intention / a decision about the future)
    \item **am going to study** (a plan decided long ago)
    \item **will take** (an uncertain idea, with *maybe / I think*)
    \item **am meeting** (present continuous — a fixed arrangement)
    \item **come out** (present simple — a fixed date / timetable)
    \item **will have sent** (future perfect — an action completed before a future point)
\end{enumerate}

### Problème 2 : Write a paragraph of predictions and intentions
\begin{enonce}
Read the notes below about a young woman called Nada and her plans. Use them to write a short paragraph of 5 to 6 sentences about her future, using a variety of future forms (be going to, will, present continuous, future perfect).
\begin{itemize}
    \item Decided: open a small bakery in her town.
    \item Arranged: visit the bank next Tuesday to ask for a loan.
    \item Her prediction: people in the area will love fresh bread.
    \item Goal: by the end of next year, learn ten new recipes.
    \item Belief: the business will not be easy at first.
\end{itemize}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A model paragraph:
*"Nada **is going to open** a small bakery in her town. Next Tuesday she **is visiting** the bank to ask for a loan. She is sure that people in the area **will love** fresh bread. By the end of next year, she **will have learned** ten new recipes. She knows the business **will not be** easy at first, but she **is going to work** hard to make it succeed."*

Notice how each form has its own job: **be going to** for firm intentions, the **present continuous** for the fixed appointment, **will** for predictions and opinions, and the **future perfect** for a result reached before a deadline.

### Problème 3 : Guided task — write your New Year resolutions
\begin{enonce}
A new year is starting. Write a short text (about 6 sentences) listing your resolutions and intentions for the year. Use **be going to** for your firm decisions, **will** for promises, and at least one **future perfect** sentence to describe a result you hope to reach by the end of the year. Choose neutral, everyday goals (studies, health, hobbies, family).
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A model answer (students may write something different but should respect the forms):
*"This year, I **am going to manage** my time better. I **am going to revise** a little every evening instead of waiting until the last week. I promise I **will not spend** so many hours on my phone. I **am also going to do** some sport twice a week to stay healthy. I **will help** my family more often at home. By the end of the year, I hope I **will have improved** my marks and **will have read** at least ten books."*

This text mixes **be going to** for decided plans, **will** for promises, and the **future perfect** (*will have improved, will have read*) for the results expected by the deadline, which is exactly what the task asks for.

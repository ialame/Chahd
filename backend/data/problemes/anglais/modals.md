### Problème 1 : The rules of a public library
\begin{enonce}
You work at the reception of a new public library and you have to explain the rules to the visitors. Using modals of obligation, prohibition and absence of obligation (must, mustn't, have to, don't have to), write the rules suggested by the notes below. Write one full sentence for each note.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Keep silent in the reading rooms. (obligation)
    \item Eating and drinking inside. (forbidden)
    \item Pay to enter. (not necessary, it is free)
    \item Show your library card to borrow a book. (obligation)
    \item Damage the books. (forbidden)
\end{itemize}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A model set of rules:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Visitors **must keep** silent in the reading rooms.
    \item You **mustn't eat** or **drink** inside the library.
    \item You **don't have to pay** to enter; the entrance is free.
    \item You **have to show** your library card to borrow a book.
    \item Readers **mustn't damage** the books.
\end{enumerate}
Notice that mustn't expresses a strict prohibition, while don't have to only means that an action is not necessary.

### Problème 2 : Giving advice to a stressed student
\begin{enonce}
Your friend writes to you because she feels very stressed before her exams. She doesn't sleep well, she revises until very late at night, and she never takes breaks. Write a short reply of 4 to 5 sentences giving her advice, using modals of advice (should, shouldn't, ought to) and, if possible, could for suggestions.
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A model reply:
*"I am sorry to hear that you feel so stressed. First, you **shouldn't** revise until very late at night, because tiredness makes learning harder. You **should** plan your work and **ought to** take short breaks every hour. You **could** also do some sport or go for a walk to relax your mind. Above all, you **should** sleep at least seven hours, so that your memory works better during the exam."*

The advice uses should and ought to for strong recommendations, shouldn't for what to avoid, and could to suggest one possible idea among others.

### Problème 3 : A small deduction puzzle
\begin{enonce}
Read the situation and answer the question using modals of deduction (must be, can't be, might be). Justify each deduction.

*"Three friends — Adam, Lina and Karim — each ordered a different drink: a coffee, a tea and an orange juice. Adam never drinks anything hot. Lina is allergic to oranges. Karim asked for a hot drink with milk."*

Who ordered which drink?
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
We reason step by step with deduction modals:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Karim asked for a hot drink with milk, so his drink **must be** the coffee (tea is usually drunk without milk here, and juice is cold).
    \item Adam never drinks anything hot, so his drink **can't be** the coffee or the tea; it **must be** the orange juice.
    \item Lina is allergic to oranges, so her drink **can't be** the juice; since the coffee and the juice are already taken, hers **must be** the tea.
\end{enumerate}
Final answer: Adam **must have** the orange juice, Lina **must have** the tea, and Karim **must have** the coffee. Each conclusion is certain because every other option has been logically eliminated.

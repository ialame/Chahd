### Problème 1 : The chain of conditions
\begin{enonce}
Read this short text, which links several first conditionals together like a chain. Then put each verb in brackets into the correct first-conditional form.

*"Karim has an important plan for the weekend. If the weather ___ (1: be) good on Saturday, he ___ (2: go) to the beach with his cousins. If they ___ (3: arrive) early, they ___ (4: find) a quiet spot near the rocks. If they ___ (5: bring) their fishing rods, they ___ (6: try) to catch some fish. And if they ___ (7: catch) enough, they ___ (8: cook) them for lunch on the sand."*
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Every link of the chain follows the same rule: **if + present simple, will + base verb**.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item is
    \item will go
    \item arrive
    \item will find
    \item bring
    \item will try
    \item catch
    \item will cook
\end{enumerate}
The whole text builds a logical chain: each condition depends on the previous one happening first.

### Problème 2 : Rewriting a list of regrets
\begin{enonce}
Sami is looking back at his school year and feels unhappy about several things. Read his real situations and rewrite each one as a regret, using **wish** or **if only**. Decide carefully whether the regret is about the present or about the past.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I am not good at maths.
    \item I did not join the football club last year.
    \item I always wake up late.
    \item I spent all my pocket money on games.
    \item I cannot play a musical instrument.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Present regrets take **wish + past simple**; past regrets take **wish + past perfect**.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I **wish I were** good at maths. (present)
    \item **If only I had joined** the football club last year. (past)
    \item I **wish I did not wake up** late every day. (present habit)
    \item I **wish I had not spent** all my pocket money on games. (past)
    \item I **wish I could play** a musical instrument. (present ability — use *could*)
\end{enumerate}
Notice that present regrets describe how things are now, while past regrets describe actions Sami can no longer change.

### Problème 3 : "What would you do if...?"
\begin{enonce}
Imagine an unreal situation and answer it in a short, guided way. Read the prompt below and write **three** full sentences in the second conditional that answer the question. Use a different verb in each sentence.

*"What would you do if you found a wallet full of money in the street?"*
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
The question is in the second conditional (*found... would do*), so every answer must keep the pattern **if + past simple, would + base verb** (or simply *would...*). A model answer:

*"If I found a wallet full of money in the street, I **would not keep** it. First, I **would look** inside for an identity card or a phone number. Then I **would take** the wallet to the nearest police station so that the owner could get it back."*

Other correct verbs would also work, as long as the second-conditional structure is respected throughout the answer.

### Problème 1 : What makes a good citizen?
\begin{enonce}
Write a short paragraph of about 6 sentences answering the question: *"What does it mean to be a good citizen today?"* Use at least four of these words: **rights, duties, responsibility, tolerance, solidarity, society**.
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A model paragraph (the words from the theme are in bold):
*"Being a good citizen means much more than living in a country. A good citizen knows both their **rights** and their **duties**, and respects the law even when nobody is watching. They take **responsibility** for their actions and try to make their **society** a fairer place. They show **tolerance** towards people of different religions, opinions and backgrounds. In times of difficulty, they offer **solidarity** to those who are weaker or in need. In short, a good citizen builds the community instead of only thinking about themselves."*

Notice how each themed word is used in a natural sentence rather than simply listed.

### Problème 2 : An opinion paragraph on equality and tolerance
\begin{enonce}
Some people say that complete equality between all citizens is impossible to reach. Write a paragraph of 6 to 8 sentences giving your opinion. Support it with at least two reasons and use the words **equality**, **discrimination**, **freedom** and **rights**.
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A model opinion paragraph:
*"In my opinion, perfect **equality** between all human beings remains a goal that is hard to reach, but it is still worth fighting for. People are born into very different conditions, and some of them suffer from **discrimination** because of their colour, gender or wealth. However, this is exactly why fair laws are needed to protect the **rights** of everyone, especially the weakest. When a society guarantees the same **freedom** and the same chances to all its members, it grows stronger and more peaceful. I therefore believe that, even if total equality is difficult, every step towards it makes the world more just. Each of us can help by treating others with respect in daily life."*

The paragraph states a clear position, gives reasons, and uses the four themed words correctly.

### Problème 3 : Re-using the vocabulary in a real situation
\begin{enonce}
Imagine your school is organising a "Human Rights Day". Write a short message of 5 to 6 sentences to invite other students, explaining why the event matters. Use at least five words from the theme: **rights, duties, democracy, tolerance, discrimination, citizen, solidarity**.
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A model invitation message:
*"Dear friends, our school is proud to organise its first Human Rights Day next Friday. The aim of this day is to remind every **citizen** of our community that **rights** and **duties** always go together. Through talks and games, we will learn how **democracy** protects our freedom and how **tolerance** helps us live together in peace. We will also discuss real examples of **discrimination** and think about how to fight it. Come and show your **solidarity** with all those who are still waiting for justice. Together, we can make our school a fairer place for everyone."*

The message is friendly, has a clear purpose, and naturally includes the themed vocabulary.

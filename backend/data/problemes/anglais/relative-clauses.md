### Problème 1 : Describe a person in one paragraph
\begin{enonce}
Here are some short notes about a person. Combine them into a single, smooth paragraph using relative clauses (who, whose, where, when). Avoid repeating the same noun again and again.
\begin{itemize}
    \item I have a cousin called Salma.
    \item She works as an engineer.
    \item Her company builds bridges.
    \item She lives in a small town.
    \item I spent every summer in that town as a child.
    \item I still remember the holidays. We played in the river then.
\end{itemize}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A model paragraph that links the notes with relative pronouns:
*"I have a cousin called Salma, **who** works as an engineer. She works for a company **whose** main job is building bridges. She lives in a small town **where** I spent every summer as a child. I still remember those holidays, **when** we played for hours in the river."*

Notice how **who** introduces extra information about Salma, **whose** shows possession (the company's job), **where** points to the place, and **when** points to the time. The clauses turn six short notes into one connected description.

### Problème 2 : Defining or non-defining in a short text
\begin{enonce}
Read this short paragraph. For each underlined relative clause, decide whether it is **defining** (essential) or **non-defining** (extra), and add commas wherever they are missing.

*"My grandmother who is now eighty-five still tells us amazing stories. She grew up in a village which had no electricity. The teacher who taught her to read worked there for free. Her favourite book which she keeps under her pillow is older than my father."*
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
We test each clause: if removing it still leaves a clear sentence, it is **non-defining** and needs commas.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item *"My grandmother**,** who is now eighty-five**,** still tells us amazing stories."* — **Non-defining**: she is already identified, so the age is extra information.
    \item *"She grew up in a village which had no electricity."* — **Defining**: it tells us which kind of village, so no commas.
    \item *"The teacher who taught her to read worked there for free."* — **Defining**: it identifies which teacher, so no commas.
    \item *"Her favourite book**,** which she keeps under her pillow**,** is older than my father."* — **Non-defining**: *her favourite book* is already specific, so the detail is extra.
\end{enumerate}
The rule: commas separate information we could remove without confusion.

### Problème 3 : Guided error correction
\begin{enonce}
A student wrote this paragraph for a writing exam. It contains five mistakes in the relative clauses. Find and correct them, then write the corrected version.

*"Casablanca is a city which it has a big port. I met some people there who their kindness surprised me. The friend which showed me around was very helpful. I visited a museum where contains old paintings. That is the morning when I will never forget it."*
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
The mistakes, one by one:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item *which it has* — the subject is repeated. Remove **it**.
    \item *who their kindness* — use **whose** for possession instead of *who their*.
    \item *The friend which* — for a person we need **who**, not *which*.
    \item *a museum where contains* — *contains* needs a subject, so use **which** (or *that*), not *where*.
    \item *the morning when I will never forget it* — remove the extra **it** at the end.
\end{enumerate}
Corrected version:
*"Casablanca is a city **which** has a big port. I met some people there **whose** kindness surprised me. The friend **who** showed me around was very helpful. I visited a museum **which** contains old paintings. That is the morning **(that)** I will never forget."*

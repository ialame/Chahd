### Problème 1 : Report a short dialogue
\begin{enonce}
Read this short conversation between Sara and a friend, then report the whole exchange in indirect speech. Use a variety of reporting verbs (said, told, asked).

*Friend: "Are you going to the library this afternoon?"*
*Sara: "Yes, I am. I have to return three books."*
*Friend: "Can you bring my dictionary back too?"*
*Sara: "Of course. I will leave it at the desk for you."*
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A possible report (notice the backshift, the *if* for the yes/no question, and *to* for the request):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Her friend asked Sara **if she was going** to the library that afternoon.
    \item Sara answered that she **was**, and that she **had to return** three books.
    \item Her friend **asked her to bring** his dictionary back too.
    \item Sara replied that of course she **would leave** it at the desk for him.
\end{enumerate}
Time markers also change: *this afternoon* becomes **that afternoon**, because the conversation is now reported as a past event.

### Problème 2 : Report a mini-interview
\begin{enonce}
A student journalist interviewed a young volunteer, Youssef, for the school newspaper. Turn the questions and answers below into a reported paragraph.

*Journalist: "Why did you join the association?"*
*Youssef: "I wanted to help children in my neighbourhood."*
*Journalist: "What do you do every weekend?"*
*Youssef: "I teach reading to younger pupils."*
*Journalist: "Will you continue next year?"*
*Youssef: "Yes, I will, because I love it."*
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A model reported version:
*"The journalist asked Youssef **why he had joined** the association. He answered that he **had wanted** to help children in his neighbourhood. Then the journalist asked him **what he did** every weekend, and Youssef explained that he **taught** reading to younger pupils. Finally, the journalist asked **whether he would continue** the next year, and Youssef replied that he **would**, because he **loved** it."*

Each question keeps its **wh- word** or uses **whether**, with **statement word order** and no auxiliary *do/did*; each answer follows the rules of **backshift**.

### Problème 3 : Turn a written message into reported speech
\begin{enonce}
Imagine your friend Lina sent you the text message below. Report its content to another classmate, as if you were telling them later what Lina wrote.

*"Hi! I can't meet you tomorrow because I am visiting my grandmother. Have you finished the project? Please send me your notes tonight. I will explain everything when I see you here on Monday."*
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A model report (statements, a yes/no question, and a request are all present):
*"Lina wrote that she **couldn't** meet me **the next day** because she **was visiting** her grandmother. She asked me **if I had finished** the project, and she **asked me to send** her my notes **that night**. She added that she **would explain** everything when she **saw** me **there** on Monday."*

The main changes are: *can't* becomes **couldn't**, *am visiting* becomes **was visiting**, *tomorrow* becomes **the next day**, *tonight* becomes **that night**, *here* becomes **there**, and the request *"please send"* becomes **asked me to send**.

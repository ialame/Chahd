English has no single "future tense": instead it uses several forms, and the one you choose depends on **how** you see the future — as a prediction, a plan, an arrangement, or a fixed schedule. This unit reviews these forms, their **uses**, and the **time markers** that go with them.

## 1. Will for the Future

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **will + base verb** (same for all persons). Negative: **won't**. Questions: **Will + subject + verb?**
**Use:** predictions about the future, **spontaneous decisions** made at the moment of speaking, promises, and offers.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- I think it **will rain** tomorrow. (prediction)
- The phone is ringing — I **'ll answer** it. (spontaneous decision)
- I promise I **won't tell** anyone. (promise)
- That bag looks heavy; I **'ll carry** it for you. (offer)
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
Use **will** when you decide **as you speak**, with no earlier plan. Verbs like *think, believe, hope, expect* and adverbs like *probably, perhaps, maybe* often introduce a prediction with **will**.
\end{remarque}

## 2. Be Going To

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **am / is / are + going to + base verb**.
**Use:** an **intention or plan** decided **before** the moment of speaking, or a **prediction based on present evidence** that you can see now.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- We **are going to visit** my grandparents this weekend. (plan decided earlier)
- She **is going to study** medicine at university. (intention)
- Look at those dark clouds — it **is going to rain**. (prediction from evidence)
\end{exemple}

## 3. Will or Be Going To?

\begin{propriete}[The key contrast]
- **Decision made now** → **will**. *The bus is late; I **'ll walk** home.*
- **Decision made before** → **be going to**. *I **'m going to walk** home — I planned it this morning.*
- **Prediction with no proof** (just an opinion) → **will**. *I think our team **will win**.*
- **Prediction with present evidence** you can point to → **be going to**. *He's driving too fast; he **'s going to crash**.*
\end{propriete}

## 4. Present Continuous for Future Arrangements

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **am / is / are + verb-ing**.
**Use:** a **fixed future arrangement** with another person — something already organised, often with a time and a place.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- I **am meeting** the dentist at 4 p.m. tomorrow.
- They **are flying** to London on Friday.
- We **aren't doing** anything special next weekend.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
**Be going to** and the **Present Continuous** are close. The Present Continuous stresses a **definite arrangement** (a fixed time/place, often with other people), while **be going to** stresses the **intention** behind the action.
\end{remarque}

## 5. Present Simple for Timetables

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** the ordinary Present Simple (base verb, **-s/-es** for he/she/it).
**Use:** future events that follow a **fixed schedule or timetable** — transport, programmes, opening hours.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- The train **leaves** at 7:15 tomorrow morning.
- The exam **starts** at 9 a.m. on Monday.
- The film **begins** at eight tonight.
\end{exemple}

## 6. Future Continuous

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **will be + verb-ing**.
**Use:** an action that will be **in progress** at a particular moment in the future.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- This time tomorrow, I **will be sitting** in the exam hall.
- Don't call at 9 — we **will be having** dinner then.
- Next week she **will be travelling** around Spain.
\end{exemple}

## 7. Future Perfect

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **will have + past participle** (the third form: finished, done, written).
**Use:** an action that will be **completed before** a certain time in the future, usually introduced by **by**.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- By next June, I **will have finished** secondary school.
- They **will have built** the new bridge by 2030.
- Call me at ten; I **will have arrived** by then.
\end{exemple}

## 8. Future Time Markers

The form you choose often goes with particular time expressions.

| Time marker | Typical form |
|---|---|
| tomorrow, next week, next year | will / be going to / Present Continuous |
| soon, in a moment | will |
| this evening, on Friday | Present Continuous (arrangement) |
| at 7:15, at 9 a.m. (schedule) | Present Simple (timetable) |
| this time tomorrow, at 8 o'clock | Future Continuous |
| by Monday, by 2030, by then | Future Perfect |
| in two hours, in a week's time | will / Future Perfect |

## Key points

\begin{propriete}[To remember]
- **will** = predictions (no proof), **spontaneous decisions**, promises and offers.
- **be going to** = plans/intentions decided before, and predictions **based on present evidence**.
- **Present Continuous** = a **fixed arrangement** (time + place, other people); **Present Simple** = a **timetable**.
- **Future Continuous** (will be + -ing) = in progress at a future moment; **Future Perfect** (will have + past participle) = completed **by** a future time.
- Watch the markers: **by** signals the Future Perfect; **this time tomorrow** signals the Future Continuous.
\end{propriete}

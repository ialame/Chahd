In the **active voice**, the subject does the action: *The teacher corrects the exams.* In the **passive voice**, the subject **receives** the action: *The exams are corrected.* This unit reviews how to **form** the passive, **when** to use it, and how to **transform** active sentences into passive ones.

## 1. The General Form: be + past participle

\begin{definition}[Form]
The passive is built with the verb **be** (in the right tense) + the **past participle** (the third form: done, made, written).
**Active:** subject + verb + object → **Passive:** object becomes the subject + **be** + past participle (+ **by** + agent).
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- **Active:** Somebody **cleans** the room every day.
- **Passive:** The room **is cleaned** every day.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
Only **transitive verbs** (verbs that take an object) can be made passive. A verb like *arrive* or *sleep* has no object, so it has no passive form.
\end{remarque}

## 2. The Passive in Different Tenses

\begin{definition}[Be changes, the past participle stays]
To put a sentence in the passive, you keep the **same tense** by changing the form of **be**; the past participle never changes.
\end{definition}

| Tense | Active | Passive (be + past participle) |
|-------|--------|--------------------------------|
| Present Simple | They **make** cars. | Cars **are made**. |
| Past Simple | They **built** the bridge. | The bridge **was built**. |
| Present Perfect | They **have sent** the letter. | The letter **has been sent**. |
| Future (will) | They **will sign** the contract. | The contract **will be signed**. |
| Present Continuous | They **are repairing** the road. | The road **is being repaired**. |

\begin{exemple}
- This dish **is prepared** with fresh fish. (present simple)
- The museum **was opened** in 1990. (past simple)
- Many trees **have been planted** this year. (present perfect)
- The results **will be announced** tomorrow. (future)
\end{exemple}

## 3. The Modal Passive

\begin{definition}[modal + be + past participle]
After a modal verb (can, must, should, may, might), the passive is formed with **modal + be + past participle**.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- The work **can be done** before Friday.
- The exam **must be taken** in June.
- This form **should be filled** in black ink.
- Mistakes **may be corrected** in pencil.
\end{exemple}

## 4. When Do We Use the Passive?

\begin{propriete}[Three main reasons]
- The **agent is unknown**: *My bike **was stolen** last night.* (we don't know who)
- The **agent is obvious or unimportant**: *The criminal **was arrested**.* (clearly by the police)
- We want to focus on the **action or the result**, not on who did it: *Penicillin **was discovered** in 1928.*
\end{propriete}

\begin{remarque}
The passive is common in **formal**, **scientific** and **news** English, where the action matters more than the person doing it: *The sample **was heated** to 100 degrees.*
\end{remarque}

## 5. The Agent: "by + agent" and Its Omission

\begin{definition}[Mentioning the doer]
When we want to say **who** does the action, we add **by + agent** at the end: *The novel **was written by** Tolstoy.*
\end{definition}

\begin{propriete}[Leave out the agent when...]
- it is **unknown**: *The window **was broken**.* (not *by somebody*)
- it is **obvious**: *He **was sent** to prison.* (by the judge — obvious)
- it is a **general "people"**: *English **is spoken** here.* (not *by people*)
\end{propriete}

\begin{exemple}
- **Active:** Picasso **painted** this picture. → **Passive:** This picture **was painted by** Picasso. (agent kept — it is important)
- **Active:** Someone **stole** my phone. → **Passive:** My phone **was stolen**. (agent dropped — unknown)
\end{exemple}

## 6. Transforming Active into Passive

\begin{propriete}[Three steps]
- 1. The **object** of the active sentence becomes the **subject** of the passive.
- 2. Put **be** in the **same tense** as the active verb, then add the **past participle**.
- 3. The old **subject** becomes the **agent** with **by** (or is dropped).
\end{propriete}

\begin{exemple}
- **Active:** The chef (subject) **cooks** (verb) the meal (object).
- **Passive:** The meal (subject) **is cooked** (be + past participle) **by the chef** (agent).
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
When the active verb has **two objects**, either can become the subject: *They **gave** me a prize* → *I **was given** a prize* / *A prize **was given** to me.*
\end{remarque}

## 7. The Passive of Reporting Verbs

\begin{definition}[Two useful patterns]
With verbs of **saying/believing** (say, believe, think, know, report), the passive has two forms:
- **It + is/was + past participle + that...**: *It **is said that** he is rich.*
- **Subject + is/was + past participle + to + infinitive**: *He **is said to** be rich.*
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- People say that she is a genius. → **It is said that** she is a genius. / She **is said to** be a genius.
- They believe that the team will win. → **It is believed that** the team will win. / The team **is believed to** win.
- They reported that the road was closed. → **It was reported that** the road was closed. / The road **was reported to** be closed.
\end{exemple}

## Key points

\begin{propriete}[To remember]
- The passive = **be (in the right tense) + past participle**; only **transitive verbs** can be passive.
- Keep the **same tense** by changing **be**; the **past participle never changes**.
- Modal passive = **modal + be + past participle** (*can be done, must be done*).
- Use the passive when the agent is **unknown, obvious, or unimportant**, or to focus on the **action/result**.
- Add **by + agent** only when the doer is important; **omit** it otherwise.
- Reporting verbs give **It is said that...** and **He is said to...**.
\end{propriete}

Good writing is not just a list of ideas — it is ideas **joined together** clearly. **Connectors** (linking words) show the reader the relationship between sentences: addition, contrast, cause, result, purpose or sequence. This unit reviews the connectors you need for the Bac, with special attention to the **construction** they require.

## 1. Addition

\begin{definition}[Adding ideas]
Addition connectors join one idea to **another idea of the same kind**. The most common are **and**, **moreover**, **furthermore**, **in addition**, **besides**.
\end{definition}

| Connector | Joins | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **and** | two words/clauses | She is clever **and** hard-working. |
| **moreover / furthermore** | a stronger second point | The plan is cheap. **Moreover**, it is safe. |
| **in addition / besides** | extra information | He speaks French. **In addition**, he knows Spanish. |

\begin{exemple}
- Tourism creates jobs, **and** it brings money to the region.
- The course is useful. **Furthermore**, it is free.
- **Besides** being tired, I was hungry.
\end{exemple}

## 2. Contrast

\begin{definition}[Showing opposition]
Contrast connectors signal a difference or a surprise between two ideas: **but**, **however**, **although / though / even though**, **despite / in spite of**, **whereas / while**, **on the other hand**.
\end{definition}

| Connector | Construction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **but** | clause **,** but **clause** | It was cold, **but** we went out. |
| **however** | sentence. **However**, sentence. | It rained. **However**, the match continued. |
| **although / even though** | + **subject + verb** | **Although** he was tired, he kept working. |
| **despite / in spite of** | + **noun / -ing** | **Despite** the rain, we went out. |
| **whereas / while** | comparing two facts | My brother likes sport, **whereas** I prefer reading. |
| **on the other hand** | a second viewpoint | Cities are exciting; **on the other hand**, they are noisy. |

\begin{remarque}
This is a very common Bac trap. **Although / though / even though** are followed by a **clause** (subject + verb). **Despite / in spite of** are followed by a **noun** or **-ing form**, never by a full clause.
- **Although** the weather **was** bad, we travelled. (clause)
- **Despite** the bad weather, we travelled. (noun)
- **In spite of** **being** ill, she came. (-ing)
\end{remarque}

## 3. Cause and Reason

\begin{definition}[Explaining why]
Cause connectors give the **reason** for something: **because**, **since**, **as** (+ clause), and **because of / due to** (+ noun).
\end{definition}

| Connector | Construction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **because / since / as** | + **subject + verb** | We stayed home **because** it was raining. |
| **because of / due to** | + **noun** | We stayed home **because of** the rain. |

\begin{remarque}
The second key construction to master: **because** is followed by a **clause** (subject + verb), while **because of** and **due to** are followed by a **noun**.
- The match was cancelled **because** it **rained**. (clause)
- The match was cancelled **because of** the **rain**. (noun)
\end{remarque}

\begin{exemple}
- **Since** the shops were closed, we bought nothing.
- **As** she was the eldest, she helped her parents.
- The flight was delayed **due to** the fog.
\end{exemple}

## 4. Consequence and Result

\begin{definition}[Showing the result]
Result connectors introduce the **consequence** of a cause: **so**, **therefore**, **thus**, **consequently**, **as a result**.
\end{definition}

| Connector | Construction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **so** | clause **, so** clause | It was late, **so** we went home. |
| **therefore / thus / consequently** | new sentence | He failed the test. **Therefore**, he must repeat it. |
| **as a result** | new sentence | Prices rose. **As a result**, sales fell. |

\begin{remarque}
**Because** introduces the **cause**; **so / therefore** introduce the **result**. Compare: *He was ill **because** he ate too much* (cause) — *He ate too much, **so** he was ill* (result).
\end{remarque}

## 5. Purpose

\begin{definition}[Showing the aim]
Purpose connectors explain **why** something is done — the goal or intention: **so that** (+ clause), **in order to** and **to** (+ infinitive).
\end{definition}

| Connector | Construction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **to** | + **infinitive** | She studies hard **to** pass the Bac. |
| **in order to** | + **infinitive** | He left early **in order to** catch the bus. |
| **so that** | + **subject + verb** | Speak louder **so that** everyone **can** hear. |

\begin{exemple}
- I am saving money **in order to** buy a laptop.
- We arrived early **so that** we could find good seats.
\end{exemple}

## 6. Sequence

\begin{definition}[Ordering ideas]
Sequence connectors organise a text step by step: **first / first of all**, **then / next / after that**, **finally / in conclusion**.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- **First**, mix the flour and the eggs. **Then**, add the milk. **Finally**, bake the cake.
- **First of all**, I will discuss the causes. **Then** I will give solutions, and **finally** I will conclude.
\end{exemple}

## Key points

\begin{propriete}[To remember]
- **Addition**: and, moreover, furthermore, in addition, besides.
- **Contrast**: but, however, whereas; **although/even though + clause** but **despite/in spite of + noun/-ing**.
- **Cause**: **because/since/as + clause** but **because of/due to + noun**.
- **Result**: so, therefore, thus, consequently, as a result.
- **Purpose**: **to / in order to + infinitive** but **so that + clause**.
- **Sequence**: first, then, finally — to order a paragraph clearly.
\end{propriete}

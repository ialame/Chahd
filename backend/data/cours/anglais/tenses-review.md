A solid command of the basic tenses is the foundation of good English. This unit reviews the **present** and **past** tenses you need for the Bac: their **form**, their **use**, and the **time markers** that signal them.

## 1. The Present Simple

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** base verb (add **-s/-es** for he/she/it). Negative and questions use **do/does**.
**Use:** habits and routines, general truths and facts, timetables.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- She **works** in a hospital. — They **don't live** in Rabat. — **Does** he **speak** English?
- Water **boils** at 100 degrees. (general truth)
- The train **leaves** at 7 a.m. (timetable)
\end{exemple}

**Time markers:** always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day, on Mondays.

## 2. The Present Continuous

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **am / is / are + verb-ing**.
**Use:** an action happening **now**, a temporary situation, or a **fixed future arrangement**.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- Look! The children **are playing** in the garden. (now)
- I **am reading** an interesting novel these days. (temporary)
- We **are meeting** the teacher tomorrow. (future arrangement)
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
**Stative verbs** (like, know, want, believe, understand) are normally **not** used in the continuous: say *I **know** the answer*, not *I am knowing*.
\end{remarque}

## 3. The Past Simple

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** regular verbs add **-ed**; irregular verbs have special forms (go → went, see → saw). Negative/questions use **did**.
**Use:** a **finished action** at a definite time in the past.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- He **visited** Marrakech last summer. — They **didn't come** to the party. — **Did** you **see** the film?
- Ibn Battuta **travelled** across many countries.
\end{exemple}

**Time markers:** yesterday, last week, two days ago, in 2010, when I was young.

## 4. The Past Continuous

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **was / were + verb-ing**.
**Use:** an action **in progress** at a moment in the past; often interrupted by a shorter action (Past Simple), with **when** and **while**.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- At 8 p.m. I **was studying** for my exam.
- **While** she **was cooking**, the phone **rang**.
- They **were watching** TV **when** the lights went out.
\end{exemple}

## 5. The Present Perfect

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **have / has + past participle** (the third form: gone, seen, done).
**Use:** a past action **connected to the present** — recent events, life experience, or an action in an **unfinished** time period.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- I **have finished** my homework. (it is done now)
- She **has visited** three countries. (life experience)
- We **have lived** here **for** ten years / **since** 2015. (still true now)
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
Useful words: **for** + a period (for two hours), **since** + a starting point (since Monday), **ever/never**, **just**, **already**, **yet** (in negatives and questions).
\end{remarque}

## 6. Present Perfect or Past Simple?

\begin{propriete}[The key contrast]
- **Past Simple** = a **finished time** that is over (yesterday, last year, in 2010). *I **saw** her yesterday.*
- **Present Perfect** = an **unfinished time** or a link to now (today, this week, ever, recently). *I **have seen** her today.*
\end{propriete}

## Key points

\begin{propriete}[To remember]
- **Present Simple** = habits/facts; **Present Continuous** = now/temporary.
- **Past Simple** = finished past at a definite time; **Past Continuous** = action in progress (when/while).
- **Present Perfect** (have/has + past participle) links the past to the **present**; use **for/since**, **already/yet/just**.
- A finished time word (yesterday, ago, last) requires the **Past Simple**, never the Present Perfect.
\end{propriete}

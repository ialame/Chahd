Relative clauses let us add information about a noun without starting a new sentence. They are introduced by a **relative pronoun** (who, which, that, whose, where, when, whom) and are essential for writing clear, connected English at the Bac level.

## 1. Relative pronouns

\begin{definition}[The main relative pronouns]
A relative pronoun joins a clause to the noun it describes (the **antecedent**).
- **who** → people: *the teacher **who** helps us*
- **which** → things/animals: *the book **which** I bought*
- **that** → people or things (informal): *the man **that** called*
- **whose** → possession: *the girl **whose** bag was stolen*
- **where** → places: *the city **where** I was born*
- **when** → time: *the day **when** we met*
- **whom** → object, formal: *the person **to whom** I wrote*
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- I know a doctor **who** speaks four languages. (person)
- This is the phone **which** I want. (thing)
- She lives in a house **whose** roof is red. (possession)
- That is the school **where** my brother studies. (place)
\end{exemple}

## 2. Defining relative clauses

\begin{definition}[Essential information]
A **defining** relative clause gives information we **need** to identify the noun. It is **not** separated by commas. Without it, the sentence loses its meaning.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- The student **who won the prize** is my friend. (which student? the one who won)
- The car **that broke down** belongs to Karim.
- People **who exercise regularly** stay healthy.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
In defining clauses, **that** can replace **who** or **which**: *the book **that** (= which) I read*. This is very common in everyday English.
\end{remarque}

## 3. Non-defining relative clauses

\begin{definition}[Extra information]
A **non-defining** relative clause gives **extra**, non-essential information. It is placed **between commas**. If we remove it, the sentence still makes complete sense.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- My father, **who is a teacher**, works in Fez.
- Casablanca, **which is the largest city**, is on the coast.
- Mr Alami, **whose son is my classmate**, lives next door.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
In non-defining clauses, **that** is **impossible**, and the pronoun can **never** be omitted. We must use who, which, whose, etc.
\end{remarque}

## 4. Defining vs non-defining: the contrast

\begin{propriete}[Compare carefully]
- **Defining** (no commas, identifies): *My sister **who lives in Rabat** is a nurse.* → I have several sisters; this one lives in Rabat.
- **Non-defining** (commas, adds a detail): *My sister, **who lives in Rabat**, is a nurse.* → I have only one sister, and by the way she lives in Rabat.
- Defining: that allowed, pronoun can be dropped.
- Non-defining: that forbidden, pronoun kept, commas required.
\end{propriete}

## 5. Omitting the relative pronoun

\begin{definition}[When the pronoun is the object]
In a **defining** clause, we can **omit** the relative pronoun when it is the **object** of the clause (there is already another subject after it).
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- The film (**that/which**) I watched was great. (I = subject, so the pronoun can go)
- The man (**who/whom**) we met is a journalist.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
We can **not** omit the pronoun when it is the **subject**: *the man **who** called* (we cannot drop **who** here). We also never omit it in non-defining clauses.
\end{remarque}

## 6. Prepositions in relative clauses

\begin{definition}[Two positions for the preposition]
When the relative clause contains a preposition, English offers two structures:
- **Informal:** preposition at the **end**, pronoun often omitted: *the man (who) I spoke **to***.
- **Formal:** preposition **before** whom/which: *the man **to whom** I spoke*.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- The house (**which**) we live **in** is old. = The house **in which** we live is old.
- The friend (**who**) I rely **on** is honest. = The friend **on whom** I rely is honest.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
In the **formal** structure we use **whom** for people and **which** for things; **that** and the omitted pronoun are **not** possible after the preposition (we never say *to that*).
\end{remarque}

## Key points

\begin{propriete}[To remember]
- Use **who** for people, **which** for things, **whose** for possession, **where** for places, **when** for time, **whom** as a formal object.
- **Defining** clauses are essential and take **no commas**; **that** is allowed and the object pronoun can be **dropped**.
- **Non-defining** clauses add extra information **between commas**; **that** is **impossible** and the pronoun is **never** omitted.
- Omit the pronoun only when it is the **object** of a defining clause, never when it is the subject.
- Prepositions go either at the **end** (informal) or before **whom/which** (formal): *the man (who) I spoke **to*** = *the man **to whom** I spoke*.
\end{propriete}

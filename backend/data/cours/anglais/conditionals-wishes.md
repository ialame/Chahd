Conditional sentences let us talk about possibilities and their results: what is always true, what is likely, what is only imaginary, and what we regret about the past. This unit reviews the **four conditionals**, the word **unless**, and the **wish/if only** structures you need for the Bac.

## 1. The Zero Conditional

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **if + present simple, present simple**.
**Use:** general truths, scientific facts, and things that are **always** true. Here **if** means *whenever* or *every time*.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- If you **heat** ice, it **melts**.
- If a plant **doesn't get** water, it **dies**.
- If I **am** tired, I **go** to bed early.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
You can replace **if** with **when** without changing the meaning much: *When you heat ice, it melts.*
\end{remarque}

## 2. The First Conditional

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **if + present simple, will + base verb**.
**Use:** a **real and probable** condition in the future — something likely to happen if the condition is met.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- If you **study** hard, you **will pass** the Bac.
- If it **rains** tomorrow, we **will stay** at home.
- If she **doesn't hurry**, she **will miss** the bus.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
The **if-clause** uses the present, **never will**: say *If it **rains*** (not *if it will rain*). You may also use **can, may, must** or an imperative in the result: *If you finish early, **you can** go home.*
\end{remarque}

## 3. The Second Conditional

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **if + past simple, would + base verb**.
**Use:** an **unreal, imaginary or improbable** situation in the **present or future** — a dream, a piece of advice, or something unlikely.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- If I **won** the lottery, I **would travel** the world.
- If I **were** you, I **would accept** the job. (advice)
- If she **studied** more, she **would get** better marks.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
With the verb **be**, we use **were** for all persons in careful English: *If I **were** rich..., If he **were** here...* You can also use **could** or **might** instead of would.
\end{remarque}

## 4. The Third Conditional

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **if + past perfect, would have + past participle**.
**Use:** an **unreal situation in the past** — something that did **not** happen. It often expresses **regret** or **criticism** about the past.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- If I **had studied**, I **would have passed** the exam. (but I didn't study, so I failed)
- If they **had left** earlier, they **wouldn't have missed** the train.
- If you **had told** me, I **would have helped** you.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
Nothing in a third conditional can be changed now — it describes a past that is over. **Could have** and **might have** are also possible in the result clause.
\end{remarque}

## 5. Summary Table of the Four Conditionals

\begin{propriete}[The four conditionals at a glance]

| Type | If-clause | Main clause | Use |
|------|-----------|-------------|-----|
| **Zero** | if + present | present | general truth, always true |
| **First** | if + present | will + verb | real, probable future |
| **Second** | if + past simple | would + verb | unreal/improbable present or future |
| **Third** | if + past perfect | would have + past participle | unreal past, regret |

\end{propriete}

## 6. Unless

\begin{definition}[Meaning]
**Unless** means **if not** / **except if**. It introduces the only condition that would change the result.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- You **won't pass unless** you **work** hard. (= if you don't work hard)
- **Unless** it **rains**, we **will** go to the beach. (= if it doesn't rain)
- I won't lend you money **unless** you **promise** to pay me back.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
After **unless**, the verb is in the **affirmative**, because *unless* already carries the negative meaning. Do not say *unless you don't work*.
\end{remarque}

## 7. Wish and If only (present and past regrets)

\begin{definition}[Wish + past simple]
**Form:** **wish / if only + past simple**.
**Use:** a regret or desire about a **present** situation we would like to be different.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- I **wish** I **had** more free time. (but I don't)
- She **wishes** she **knew** the answer.
- **If only** I **were** taller!
\end{exemple}

\begin{definition}[Wish + past perfect]
**Form:** **wish / if only + past perfect**.
**Use:** a regret about the **past** — something we are sorry happened or did not happen.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- I **wish** I **had studied** harder for the test. (but I didn't)
- He **wishes** he **hadn't said** that.
- **If only** we **had arrived** earlier!
\end{exemple}

## 8. Wish + would

\begin{definition}[Form and use]
**Form:** **wish + would + base verb**.
**Use:** to complain about an **annoying habit** or to express a **desire for change** in someone else's behaviour or in a situation.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- I **wish** you **would stop** making noise.
- I **wish** it **would stop** raining.
- She **wishes** her brother **would help** in the house.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
We do not use **wish + would** about ourselves; for our own situation we use **wish + past simple**: say *I wish I **had** a car* (not *I wish I would have*).
\end{remarque}

## Key points

\begin{propriete}[To remember]
- **Zero** (if + present, present) = always true; **First** (if + present, will) = real future.
- **Second** (if + past, would) = unreal present; **Third** (if + past perfect, would have + participle) = unreal past/regret.
- The **if-clause never uses will or would**: say *If it rains*, not *if it will rain*.
- Use **were** for all persons after *if* and *wish*: *If I **were** you; I wish I **were** rich.*
- **Unless** = *if not*, and is followed by an **affirmative** verb.
- **wish + past simple** = present regret; **wish + past perfect** = past regret; **wish + would** = complaint about someone's habit.
\end{propriete}

In the F and G sections of the national exam you are asked to **complete the exchanges**: short conversations where you choose or write the missing line. To succeed, you must recognise the **language function** behind each turn — is the speaker offering help, complaining, apologising, agreeing? This unit reviews the most common functions, their usual expressions, and a short original dialogue for each.

## 1. Offering and Asking for Help

\begin{definition}[How to express it]
**Offering help:** Can I help you? — Shall I give you a hand? — Would you like me to ...? — Let me carry that for you.
**Asking for help:** Could you help me, please? — Can you give me a hand? — Would you mind helping me with ...?
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- A: You look lost. **Can I help you find** the bus station?
- B: Yes, please! I have been walking in circles for ten minutes.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
An **offer** is accepted with *Yes, please / That's very kind of you* and refused politely with *No, thank you. I can manage*.
\end{remarque}

## 2. Making, Accepting and Refusing Suggestions

\begin{definition}[How to express it]
**Suggesting:** Why don't we ...? — How about + verb-ing? — Let's ... — We could ... — Shall we ...?
**Accepting:** Good idea! — That sounds great. — Why not!
**Refusing:** I'd rather not. — I'm not really in the mood. — Sorry, that doesn't suit me.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- A: **How about revising** for the Bac together this weekend?
- B: **Good idea!** Let's meet at the library on Saturday morning.
\end{exemple}

## 3. Complaining

\begin{definition}[How to express it]
**Complaining:** I'm sorry to say this, but ... — I'm afraid there's a problem with ... — This isn't good enough. — I'd like to complain about ... — Excuse me, but this ... doesn't work.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- A: Excuse me, **I'd like to complain about** my meal. The soup is cold.
- B: I'm terribly sorry, sir. I'll bring you a hot one right away.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
A complaint usually opens politely (*Excuse me / I'm sorry, but*) before stating the problem. The natural reply is an **apology** plus a solution.
\end{remarque}

## 4. Apologising

\begin{definition}[How to express it]
**Apologising:** I'm (so/terribly) sorry. — I do apologise. — Please forgive me. — It was my fault. — I didn't mean to ...
**Responding:** Never mind. — It doesn't matter. — That's all right. — Don't worry about it.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- A: **I'm so sorry** I broke your pen. I'll buy you a new one.
- B: **Never mind**, it was old anyway.
\end{exemple}

## 5. Giving Advice

\begin{definition}[How to express it]
**Advising:** You should/shouldn't ... — If I were you, I'd ... — Why don't you ...? — You'd better ... — I'd advise you to ... — It might be a good idea to ...
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- A: I feel so tired before every exam.
- B: **If I were you, I'd** go to bed earlier and stop drinking coffee at night.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
**You'd better** (= had better) is stronger than *should*; it suggests a warning: *You'd better hurry or you'll miss the train.*
\end{remarque}

## 6. Agreeing and Disagreeing

\begin{definition}[How to express it]
**Agreeing:** I agree. — That's true. — You're absolutely right. — Exactly! — So do I. / Neither do I.
**Disagreeing:** I'm afraid I disagree. — I don't think so. — That's not quite true. — I see your point, but ...
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- A: I think social media wastes too much of our time.
- B: **You're absolutely right.** I check my phone far too often.
\end{exemple}

## 7. Expressing an Opinion

\begin{definition}[How to express it]
**Giving an opinion:** In my opinion, ... — I think/believe (that) ... — As far as I'm concerned, ... — If you ask me, ... — From my point of view, ...
**Asking for one:** What do you think of ...? — How do you feel about ...?
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- A: **What do you think of** studying abroad?
- B: **In my opinion**, it's a great chance to learn a new culture.
\end{exemple}

## 8. Reacting to News

\begin{definition}[How to express it]
**Good news:** That's wonderful! — Congratulations! — I'm so happy for you! — Well done!
**Bad news:** Oh no! — I'm so sorry to hear that. — What a pity! — That's terrible.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- A: I passed my Bac with honours!
- B: **Congratulations!** I'm so happy for you. You really deserve it.
\end{exemple}

## 9. Asking for and Giving Permission

\begin{definition}[How to express it]
**Asking:** Can/Could I ...? — May I ...? — Do you mind if I ...? — Is it all right if I ...?
**Giving:** Yes, of course. — Sure, go ahead. — Certainly. — Not at all.
**Refusing:** I'm afraid you can't. — Sorry, that's not allowed.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- A: **May I** open the window? It's very hot in here.
- B: **Of course, go ahead.**
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
With *Do you mind if I ...?*, agreeing means saying **No** (= I don't mind): *No, not at all.* A *Yes* would sound like a refusal.
\end{remarque}

## 10. Inviting

\begin{definition}[How to express it]
**Inviting:** Would you like to ...? — How about coming to ...? — Why don't you join us? — Are you free to ...?
**Accepting:** I'd love to. — That would be lovely. — Thanks, I'd be glad to.
**Declining:** I'd love to, but ... — Thanks, but I'm afraid I can't.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- A: **Would you like to** come to my birthday party on Friday?
- B: **I'd love to!** What time does it start?
\end{exemple}

## Method: How to Complete an Exchange

\begin{propriete}[Step by step]
- **Read both lines.** The answer must fit what comes **before** and **after** the gap.
- **Identify the function** from clues: a problem signals a **complaint**; *Thank you* signals an **offer/help**; good news signals **congratulations**.
- **Match the reply to its pair:** an apology answers a complaint; *Yes, please* answers an offer; *Congratulations* answers good news.
- **Watch the grammar:** *Do you mind if I ...?* is answered with **No**, not Yes.
- **Stay polite and natural** — exam answers favour the polite, common expression.
\end{propriete}

## Key points

\begin{propriete}[To remember]
- Each exchange tests a **function**: help, suggestion, complaint, apology, advice, agreement, opinion, news, permission, invitation.
- An **offer** is *Can I help you?*; a **request** is *Could you help me?*
- A **complaint** is met with an **apology** plus a solution.
- **Should / If I were you / You'd better** give advice; **You'd better** is the strongest.
- *Do you mind if I ...?* → agree by saying **No, not at all.**
- Always pick the **polite, natural** answer that links the line before to the line after.
\end{propriete}

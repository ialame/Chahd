When we tell someone what another person said, we often use **reported speech** (also called *indirect speech*) instead of repeating the exact words. This unit shows how to report **statements**, **questions** and **commands**, with the tense changes and the changes of time and place words you need for the Bac.

## 1. Direct and Reported Speech

\begin{definition}[The two ways of reporting]
**Direct speech** gives the exact words inside quotation marks: *She said, "I am tired."*
**Reported speech** tells the message without quotation marks, usually with a reporting verb (**say, tell, ask**) and often the word **that**: *She said **that** she was tired.*
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- Direct: He said, "I work in Casablanca."
- Reported: He said **that** he **worked** in Casablanca.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
After **tell** we name the listener; after **say** we do not. We say *She **told me** that...* but *She **said** that...* (not *she said me*).
\end{remarque}

## 2. The Backshift of Tenses

\begin{definition}[The rule]
When the reporting verb is in the **past** (said, told, asked), the verb in the reported clause usually **moves one step back** into the past. This is called the **backshift**.
\end{definition}

| Direct speech | Reported speech |
| --- | --- |
| Present Simple (work) | Past Simple (worked) |
| Present Continuous (am working) | Past Continuous (was working) |
| Past Simple (worked) | Past Perfect (had worked) |
| Present Perfect (have worked) | Past Perfect (had worked) |
| will | would |
| can | could |
| may | might |
| must | had to |

\begin{exemple}
- "I **am** busy." → She said she **was** busy.
- "We **will** help you." → They said they **would** help us.
- "I **can** swim." → He said he **could** swim.
- "I **have finished**." → She said she **had finished**.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
The backshift is **not** needed when we report a **general truth** or something **still true**: *The teacher said the Earth **goes** round the Sun.* Also, **would, could, might, should** and **had** usually do not change.
\end{remarque}

## 3. Changes of Time and Place Words

\begin{definition}[Words that point to the moment of speaking]
Words like **now, today, here, this** point to the speaker's time and place. When we report later, these words usually **change** to fit the new situation.
\end{definition}

| Direct | Reported |
| --- | --- |
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| yesterday | the day before / the previous day |
| last week | the week before |
| next week | the following week |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |

\begin{exemple}
- "I will see you **tomorrow**." → He said he would see me **the next day**.
- "I bought **this** book **here**." → She said she had bought **that** book **there**.
\end{exemple}

## 4. Reported Questions

\begin{definition}[How questions change]
In reported questions we keep the **normal word order** (subject + verb), use **no** auxiliary *do/does/did*, and use **no** question mark. We also apply the backshift.
\end{definition}

\begin{propriete}[Two types]
- **Wh- questions** keep the question word (what, where, when, why, how): *"Where do you live?" → She asked me **where** I **lived**.*
- **Yes/No questions** start with **if** or **whether**: *"Do you speak English?" → He asked me **if** I **spoke** English.*
\end{propriete}

\begin{exemple}
- "What **are** you doing?" → She asked what I **was** doing.
- "**Did** you see the film?" → He asked **whether** I **had seen** the film.
- "Why **is** she crying?" → They asked why she **was** crying.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
A common mistake is to keep the question order: write *He asked where **I lived**, not He asked where **did I live**.*
\end{remarque}

## 5. Reported Commands and Requests

\begin{definition}[The structure]
For orders and requests we use **tell / ask + object + (not) to + infinitive**. There is no backshift because we use an infinitive.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- "Open the window." → She **told** me **to open** the window.
- "Please help me." → He **asked** me **to help** him.
- "Don't be late." → The teacher **told** us **not to be** late.
\end{exemple}

## 6. Reporting Verbs

\begin{propriete}[Useful verbs and their patterns]
- **say / tell** -- for statements (*She said that... / She told me that...*).
- **ask** -- for questions and requests (*He asked if... / He asked me to...*).
- **advise** + object + to (*She advised me to rest.*).
- **suggest** + verb-ing or that-clause (*He suggested going out / suggested that we leave.*).
- **order, warn, remind, invite** also take *object + to + infinitive*.
\end{propriete}

\begin{exemple}
- "You should study harder." → The teacher **advised** me **to study** harder.
- "Let's visit the museum." → He **suggested visiting** the museum.
\end{exemple}

## Key points

\begin{propriete}[To remember]
- After a past reporting verb, **backshift** the tense one step (present → past, will → would, can → could, must → had to).
- Change time and place words: **now → then, today → that day, tomorrow → the next day, here → there, this → that**.
- Reported **questions** use normal word order, **no** *do/does/did*, and **if/whether** for yes/no questions.
- Reported **commands/requests** use **tell/ask + (not) to + infinitive**.
- Use **tell** with a listener (*told me*), **say** without one (*said that*); do not backshift a general truth.
\end{propriete}

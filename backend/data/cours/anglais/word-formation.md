At the National Exam you are often asked to **put the word between brackets in the correct form**. To do this well you need two skills: knowing the **suffixes and prefixes** that build new words, and reading the **sentence** to decide which part of speech is needed — a noun, a verb, an adjective or an adverb.

## 1. The four parts of speech you must recognise

\begin{definition}[Noun, verb, adjective, adverb]
- A **noun** names a thing, person or idea (*decision, kindness, teacher*). It often follows *a / the / this / his* or an adjective.
- A **verb** expresses an action or state (*decide, modernise, widen*). It often follows a subject (*they ___ the plan*).
- An **adjective** describes a noun (*useful, helpless, creative*). It often comes before a noun or after *be / seem / look*.
- An **adverb** describes a verb, an adjective or another adverb (*quickly, carefully*). It often answers *how?* and usually ends in **-ly**.
\end{definition}

\begin{remarque}
The same root can give all four forms. From **care**: the noun *care*, the verb *care*, the adjective *careful*, the adverb *carefully*. Your job is to pick the one the sentence needs.
\end{remarque}

## 2. Suffixes that form NOUNS

\begin{propriete}[Common noun suffixes]
- **-tion / -sion**: educate → **education**, decide → **decision**, discuss → **discussion**.
- **-ment**: develop → **development**, agree → **agreement**, employ → **employment**.
- **-ness**: kind → **kindness**, happy → **happiness**, dark → **darkness**.
- **-ity**: able → **ability**, active → **activity**, real → **reality**.
- **-er / -or** (the person who does something): teach → **teacher**, act → **actor**, visit → **visitor**.
\end{propriete}

\begin{exemple}
- The government invested in **education** (educate). — *education* is a noun after *the*.
- Their **agreement** (agree) ended the conflict.
- We admire her **kindness** (kind) towards others.
- He is a famous **actor** (act).
\end{exemple}

## 3. Suffixes that form ADJECTIVES

\begin{propriete}[Common adjective suffixes]
- **-ful** (full of): use → **useful**, care → **careful**, hope → **hopeful**.
- **-less** (without): use → **useless**, care → **careless**, home → **homeless**.
- **-ive**: create → **creative**, act → **active**, attract → **attractive**.
- **-al**: nature → **natural**, education → **educational**, culture → **cultural**.
- **-ous**: danger → **dangerous**, fame → **famous**, ambition → **ambitious**.
- **-able / -ible**: rely → **reliable**, comfort → **comfortable**, sense → **sensible**.
\end{propriete}

\begin{exemple}
- This tool is very **useful** (use). — adjective before *tool* is implied, after *very*.
- She is a **creative** (create) student.
- Smoking is **dangerous** (danger) for your health.
- He gave a **careless** (care) answer and lost marks.
\end{exemple}

## 4. Suffixes that form VERBS

\begin{propriete}[Common verb suffixes]
- **-ise / -ize** (make / turn into): modern → **modernise**, real → **realise**, special → **specialise**.
- **-en** (make more): wide → **widen**, strong → **strengthen**, short → **shorten**, deep → **deepen**.
\end{propriete}

\begin{exemple}
- They want to **modernise** (modern) the school.
- We must **strengthen** (strong) our ties with neighbours.
- Reading will **widen** (wide) your knowledge.
\end{exemple}

\begin{remarque}
British English prefers **-ise** (organise), American English prefers **-ize** (organize). Both are accepted in the exam, but stay consistent.
\end{remarque}

## 5. The suffix that forms ADVERBS: -ly

\begin{definition}[Adjective + -ly]
Most adverbs are made by adding **-ly** to an adjective: quick → **quickly**, careful → **carefully**, serious → **seriously**.
Spelling notes: *happy → happily* (y → i), *true → truly* (drop the e), *responsible → responsibly* (le → ly).
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- She answered the question **carefully** (careful). — adverb after the verb *answered*.
- He drives too **quickly** (quick).
- They listened **attentively** (attentive) to the lesson.
\end{exemple}

## 6. Negative PREFIXES

\begin{propriete}[Prefixes that reverse the meaning]
- **un-**: happy → **unhappy**, fair → **unfair**, known → **unknown**.
- **in- / im- / ir- / il-**: correct → **incorrect**, possible → **impossible**, regular → **irregular**, legal → **illegal**.
- **dis-**: agree → **disagree**, honest → **dishonest**, appear → **disappear**.
- **mis-** (wrongly): understand → **misunderstand**, use → **misuse**, spell → **misspell**.
\end{propriete}

\begin{remarque}
The choice of *in- / im- / ir- / il-* depends on the first letter: **im-** before *m/p* (impossible, immature), **ir-** before *r* (irregular), **il-** before *l* (illegal), **in-** elsewhere (incorrect, invisible).
\end{remarque}

\begin{exemple}
- It is **impossible** (possible) to finish on time.
- They **disagree** (agree) with the decision.
- He gave an **incorrect** (correct) answer.
- Don't **misunderstand** (understand) me.
\end{exemple}

## 7. Word families

\begin{definition}[Building a word family]
A **word family** groups the noun, verb, adjective and adverb that share one root. Learn them together.
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- **educate** (verb) — **education** (noun) — **educational** (adjective) — **educator** (noun, person).
- **produce** (verb) — **production / product** (nouns) — **productive** (adjective) — **productively** (adverb).
- **innovate** (verb) — **innovation** (noun) — **innovative** (adjective) — **innovatively** (adverb).
- **decide** (verb) — **decision** (noun) — **decisive** (adjective) — **decisively** (adverb).
- **success** (noun) — **succeed** (verb) — **successful** (adjective) — **successfully** (adverb).
\end{exemple}

## 8. The method: choose the form from the sentence

\begin{propriete}[Step by step]
- **Step 1 — Find the gap's job.** What is missing: a noun, a verb, an adjective or an adverb? Look at the words around it.
- **Step 2 — Apply the clues.** After *a / the / his / an adjective* → a **noun**. After *be / seem / very* or before a noun → an **adjective**. To describe a verb → an **adverb (-ly)**. After a subject with no other verb → a **verb**.
- **Step 3 — Check the meaning.** Is it positive or negative? If negative, add a prefix (*un-, in-, dis-, mis-*).
- **Step 4 — Spell carefully.** Watch *y → i* (happy → happiness), dropped *e* (create → creation), and doubled letters.
\end{propriete}

\begin{exemple}
- "She made an important ___ (decide)." → after *an important* we need a **noun**: **decision**.
- "The machine works ___ (efficient)." → it describes the verb *works*, so we need an **adverb**: **efficiently**.
- "This information is very ___ (use)." → after *very* we need an **adjective**: **useful**.
- "Scientists want to ___ (modern) the lab." → after *to* we need a **verb**: **modernise**.
\end{exemple}

## 9. Gap-fill practice (put the word in the correct form)

\begin{exemple}
- 1. His ___ (kind) surprised everyone. → **kindness** (noun).
- 2. The team worked very ___ (hard) on the project. → **hard** (adverb, same form).
- 3. It is ___ (possible) to read in the dark. → **impossible** (negative adjective).
- 4. The new policy will ___ (strong) the economy. → **strengthen** (verb).
- 5. She is a ___ (create) and ___ (success) writer. → **creative** / **successful** (adjectives).
- 6. They reached an ___ (agree) after a long ___ (discuss). → **agreement** / **discussion** (nouns).
\end{exemple}

## Key points

\begin{propriete}[To remember]
- First decide **which part of speech** the gap needs (noun, verb, adjective, adverb), then add the right suffix.
- **Nouns**: -tion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -er/-or. **Adjectives**: -ful, -less, -ive, -al, -ous, -able. **Verbs**: -ise/-ize, -en. **Adverbs**: -ly.
- A negative meaning needs a **prefix**: un-, in-/im-/ir-/il-, dis-, mis-.
- Learn whole **word families** (educate / education / educational / educator) so every form is ready.
- Read the words **around the gap** for clues, and check your **spelling** when you add the ending.
\end{propriete}

Work shapes our lives and our societies. This unit explores the language of **employment** and **unemployment**, the phenomenon known as the **brain drain**, and the role of **international cooperation** in helping countries develop. Master the **vocabulary**, the **useful expressions**, and the **key ideas** below to write and speak confidently about these Bac themes.

## 1. Key vocabulary

| Word | Meaning / example |
|------|-------------------|
| **employment** | the state of having paid work — *Stable **employment** gives people security.* |
| **unemployment** | the situation of having no job — *Youth **unemployment** is a serious problem.* |
| **job** | a particular paid position — *She found a **job** in a bank.* |
| **career** | a person's professional life over many years — *He built a successful **career** in medicine.* |
| **skills** | abilities gained through training or practice — *Computer **skills** are essential today.* |
| **qualifications** | diplomas and certificates proving one's training — *The post requires high **qualifications**.* |
| **labour market** | the supply of and demand for workers — *The **labour market** is very competitive.* |
| **employer** | a person or company that hires workers — *A good **employer** trains its staff.* |
| **employee** | a person who works for an employer — *Each **employee** signs a contract.* |
| **migration** | the movement of people from one place to another — *Economic **migration** is increasing.* |
| **to emigrate** | to leave one's own country to live abroad — *Many graduates **emigrate** to Europe.* |
| **brain drain** | the loss of skilled people who leave their country — *The **brain drain** harms developing nations.* |
| **expertise** | special, expert knowledge or skill — *Doctors take their **expertise** abroad.* |
| **developing country** | a poorer country still building its economy — *A **developing country** needs investment.* |
| **cooperation** | working together for a common goal — *International **cooperation** fights poverty.* |
| **aid** | help, especially money or supplies, given to others — *Foreign **aid** supports schools.* |
| **NGO** | a Non-Governmental Organisation — *An **NGO** built a clinic in the village.* |
| **the United Nations** | the world organisation promoting peace and development — *The **United Nations** funds health programmes.* |
| **opportunity** | a chance to do or achieve something — *Education opens up **opportunities**.* |
| **salary** | regular pay for a job, usually monthly — *They were offered a higher **salary** abroad.* |

## 2. Useful expressions

\begin{definition}[Phrases to talk about work and cooperation]
- **to look for / to apply for a job** — *She is **applying for a job** as an engineer.*
- **to be out of work / unemployed** — *Thousands of young people **are out of work**.*
- **to make a living** — *It is hard to **make a living** in some regions.*
- **to seek better opportunities abroad** — *Graduates **seek better opportunities abroad**.*
- **to contribute to development** — *Returning experts **contribute to development**.*
- **to provide / receive aid** — *Rich countries **provide aid** to poorer ones.*
- **to bridge the gap** — *Cooperation helps to **bridge the gap** between rich and poor.*
\end{definition}

\begin{exemple}
- After graduation, he **applied for a job** but stayed **unemployed** for a year, so he decided to **seek better opportunities abroad**.
- Several **NGOs** work with **the United Nations** to **provide aid** and **bridge the gap** in education.
\end{exemple}

## 3. Key ideas

### 3.1 Youth unemployment

\begin{propriete}[A major challenge]
In many countries, young people struggle to enter the **labour market**. Even with good **qualifications**, they may not find a **job** that matches their **skills**. **Causes** include a mismatch between education and the needs of employers, slow economic growth, and a lack of **opportunities**. **Consequences** include frustration, poverty, and the temptation to **emigrate**.
\end{propriete}

### 3.2 The brain drain: causes and consequences

\begin{propriete}[When talent leaves]
The **brain drain** happens when **skilled** workers — doctors, engineers, scientists — **emigrate** to richer countries.
- **Causes:** low **salaries**, few **opportunities**, poor working conditions, and political instability at home.
- **Consequences:** the home **developing country** loses its **expertise**, while the host country gains trained workers for free. This widens the gap between nations.
\end{propriete}

\begin{remarque}
The brain drain is not always negative: workers abroad often send money home (**remittances**) and may return one day with new **skills** — a process sometimes called "**brain gain**".
\end{remarque}

### 3.3 International cooperation and development

\begin{propriete}[Working together]
**International cooperation** brings governments, **NGOs**, and **the United Nations** together to support **developing countries**. Through **aid**, education programmes, and shared **expertise**, cooperation aims to create local **employment**, reduce the brain drain, and help countries develop on their own.
\end{propriete}

\begin{exemple}
- A **developing country** that offers fair **salaries** and real **career** prospects keeps its talent at home.
- An **NGO** funded by **the United Nations** trains local teachers, giving young people **opportunities** without the need to **emigrate**.
\end{exemple}

## Key points

\begin{propriete}[To remember]
- **Employment** = having work; **unemployment** = having none. The **labour market** decides who finds a **job**.
- The **brain drain** = skilled people **emigrating** because of low **salaries** and few **opportunities**; it costs **developing countries** their **expertise**.
- **International cooperation** — through **aid**, **NGOs**, and **the United Nations** — helps countries develop and create local **opportunities**.
- Useful verbs: **apply for** a job, **make a living**, **seek opportunities abroad**, **provide aid**, **contribute to development**.
\end{propriete}

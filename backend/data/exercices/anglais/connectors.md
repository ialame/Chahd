### Exercice 1 : Choose the right connector — addition, contrast, cause

\begin{enonce}
Choose the most suitable connector from the list to complete each sentence: *moreover, however, because, so, in order to*.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item She studies very hard, ___ she is sure to pass her exams.
    \item The hotel was expensive; ___, the service was disappointing.
    \item He woke up early ___ catch the first bus to school.
    \item We cancelled the trip ___ the weather was terrible.
    \item Solar energy is clean and renewable; ___, it is becoming cheaper every year.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Each connector signals a different relationship between ideas (consequence, contrast, purpose, cause, addition).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item She studies very hard, **so** she is sure to pass her exams. (consequence)
    \item The hotel was expensive; **however**, the service was disappointing. (contrast)
    \item He woke up early **in order to** catch the first bus to school. (purpose)
    \item We cancelled the trip **because** the weather was terrible. (cause)
    \item Solar energy is clean and renewable; **moreover**, it is becoming cheaper every year. (addition)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 2 : Join two sentences with a given connector

\begin{enonce}
Join each pair of sentences using the connector in brackets. Make any necessary changes.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The match was cancelled. It started to rain heavily. (because)
    \item My brother is only sixteen. He already speaks three languages. (although)
    \item I saved some money. I wanted to buy a new laptop. (so that)
    \item The film was long. It was never boring. (but)
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
The connector replaces the full stop and shows how the two ideas are linked.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The match was cancelled **because** it started to rain heavily.
    \item **Although** my brother is only sixteen, he already speaks three languages.
    \item I saved some money **so that** I could buy a new laptop.
    \item The film was long, **but** it was never boring.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 3 : Transform *although* into *despite / in spite of*

\begin{enonce}
Rewrite each sentence using *despite* or *in spite of* instead of *although*. Remember that these are followed by a noun or a gerund, not by a full clause.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Although it was raining, we played football outside.
    \item Although she felt nervous, she gave an excellent speech.
    \item Although he was very tired, he finished his homework.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
After *despite / in spite of*, use a **noun** or a **verb + -ing**, and often add *the fact that* when you keep a clause.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **Despite the rain**, we played football outside. / **In spite of the rain**, we played football outside.
    \item **Despite feeling nervous**, she gave an excellent speech. / **In spite of feeling nervous**, she gave an excellent speech.
    \item **Despite being very tired**, he finished his homework. / **In spite of the fact that** he was very tired, he finished his homework.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 4 : Transform *because* into *because of*

\begin{enonce}
Rewrite each sentence using *because of* instead of *because*. Turn the clause into a noun phrase.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We stayed at home because the weather was bad.
    \item The flight was delayed because the fog was thick.
    \item She missed the bus because she was late.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
*Because* is followed by a **clause** (subject + verb); *because of* is followed by a **noun phrase**.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We stayed at home **because of the bad weather**.
    \item The flight was delayed **because of the thick fog**.
    \item She missed the bus **because of her lateness**. / She missed the bus **because of being late**.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 5 : Gap-fill — purpose and consequence

\begin{enonce}
Fill in each gap with one connector that expresses either purpose or consequence: *so as to, therefore, so that, as a result, in order not to*.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item He spoke quietly ___ wake the baby.
    \item The road was closed; ___, we had to take a longer route.
    \item She turned off her phone ___ she could concentrate.
    \item Prices rose sharply. ___, many families spent less.
    \item We left early ___ avoid the traffic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
*So as to / in order to* and *so that* show **purpose**; *therefore / as a result* show **consequence**.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item He spoke quietly **in order not to** wake the baby.
    \item The road was closed; **therefore**, we had to take a longer route.
    \item She turned off her phone **so that** she could concentrate.
    \item Prices rose sharply. **As a result**, many families spent less.
    \item We left early **so as to** avoid the traffic.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 6 : Contrast connectors — three structures

\begin{enonce}
Express the same idea of contrast in three different ways, using *although*, *however*, and *despite*. Start from this idea: *The team trained hard. They lost the final.*
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Use *although* (one sentence, two clauses).
    \item Use *however* (two sentences linked by a semicolon).
    \item Use *despite* (followed by a gerund).
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
The three connectors share the same meaning but follow different grammar.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **Although** the team trained hard, they lost the final.
    \item The team trained hard; **however**, they lost the final.
    \item **Despite training hard**, the team lost the final.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 7 : Correct the mistake

\begin{enonce}
Each sentence contains one mistake in the use of a connector. Correct it.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Despite he was ill, he came to school.
    \item We took our umbrellas because of it was cloudy.
    \item Although the long journey, the children stayed calm.
    \item She is intelligent, moreover she is sometimes lazy.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Watch the grammar that follows each connector: a clause after *although / because*, a noun or gerund after *despite / because of*.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **Although** he was ill, he came to school. (*despite* needs a noun/gerund, not a clause)
    \item We took our umbrellas **because** it was cloudy. (*because of* needs a noun phrase)
    \item **Despite** the long journey, the children stayed calm. (*although* needs a clause, *despite* takes the noun)
    \item She is intelligent; **however**, she is sometimes lazy. (contrast, not addition)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 8 : Match the connector to its function

\begin{enonce}
For each connector, write its function (addition, contrast, cause, consequence, or purpose) and use it in one short original sentence of your own.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item furthermore
    \item whereas
    \item since
    \item consequently
    \item so as to
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Knowing the function of a connector helps you organise ideas logically.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **furthermore** — addition: *The course is useful; furthermore, it is completely free.*
    \item **whereas** — contrast: *My sister loves the city, whereas I prefer the countryside.*
    \item **since** — cause: *Since the shops were closed, we cooked at home.*
    \item **consequently** — consequence: *He missed the deadline; consequently, his project was rejected.*
    \item **so as to** — purpose: *She whispered so as to keep the secret safe.*
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 1 : Present simple vs present continuous
\begin{enonce}
Put the verb in brackets into the present simple or the present continuous.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item My brother usually ___ (walk) to school, but today he ___ (take) the bus.
    \item Listen! The neighbours ___ (play) loud music again.
    \item Water ___ (boil) at one hundred degrees.
    \item I ___ (not / understand) this exercise at the moment.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Use the **present simple** for habits and general truths; use the **present continuous** for actions happening now.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **walks** (habit) ... **is taking** (now)
    \item **are playing** (happening now — *Listen!*)
    \item **boils** (general truth)
    \item **do not understand** (*understand* is a state verb, so no continuous form)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 2 : Past simple vs past continuous
\begin{enonce}
Put the verb in brackets into the past simple or the past continuous.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item While I ___ (study) last night, the lights suddenly ___ (go) off.
    \item We ___ (watch) a film when my phone ___ (ring).
    \item Yesterday I ___ (wake) up early and ___ (have) breakfast on the balcony.
    \item The children ___ (play) in the garden all afternoon.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Use the **past continuous** for a longer action in progress, and the **past simple** for a shorter action that interrupts it or for completed events.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **was studying** ... **went** (a shorter event interrupts a longer one)
    \item **were watching** ... **rang**
    \item **woke** ... **had** (two completed events one after the other)
    \item **were playing** (a long action covering the whole afternoon)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 3 : Present perfect vs past simple
\begin{enonce}
Put the verb in brackets into the present perfect or the past simple.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I ___ (live) in this town since 2018.
    \item She ___ (visit) Marrakech three times last year.
    \item ___ you ever ___ (try) Japanese food?
    \item We ___ (already / finish) our homework.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Use the **present perfect** for actions linked to now (with *since*, *ever*, *already*); use the **past simple** for a finished time in the past (*last year*).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **have lived** (*since 2018* — it continues now)
    \item **visited** (*last year* — a finished period)
    \item **Have** ... **tried** (*ever* — experience up to now)
    \item **have already finished**
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 4 : Match the time marker with the tense
\begin{enonce}
Match each time marker on the left with the tense it usually goes with on the right.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item right now / at the moment
    \item every morning / usually
    \item two days ago / last week
    \item since Monday / for three years / yet
\end{enumerate}
A. present simple — B. present continuous — C. past simple — D. present perfect
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item right now / at the moment → **B (present continuous)**
    \item every morning / usually → **A (present simple)**
    \item two days ago / last week → **C (past simple)**
    \item since Monday / for three years / yet → **D (present perfect)**
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 5 : Choose the correct tense
\begin{enonce}
Choose the correct verb form for each sentence and justify briefly.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Look at the sky! It (rains / is raining).
    \item My grandfather (told / has told) me that story when I was a child.
    \item I can't go out because I (don't finish / haven't finished) my project yet.
    \item The sun (rose / rises) in the east.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **is raining** — an action happening now (*Look!*).
    \item **told** — a finished moment in the past (*when I was a child*).
    \item **haven't finished** — present perfect with *yet*, linked to now.
    \item **rises** — a general truth, so present simple.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 6 : Transform from present to past
\begin{enonce}
Rewrite each sentence in the past. Change *today/now* into *yesterday* and keep the meaning of the tense (simple stays simple, continuous stays continuous).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I get up at seven and check my messages.
    \item She is reading a book in the library.
    \item They are cooking while their mother is resting.
    \item We meet our friends after class.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I **got** up at seven and **checked** my messages.
    \item She **was reading** a book in the library.
    \item They **were cooking** while their mother **was resting**.
    \item We **met** our friends after class.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 7 : Correct the mistake
\begin{enonce}
Each sentence has one tense mistake. Find it and correct it.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I have seen that film last weekend.
    \item She don't like getting up early.
    \item While we walked home, it started to rain heavily.
    \item He is knowing the answer to your question.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I **saw** that film last weekend. (*last weekend* needs the past simple, not the present perfect)
    \item She **doesn't** like getting up early. (third person needs *doesn't*)
    \item While we **were walking** home, it started to rain heavily. (the long background action takes the past continuous)
    \item He **knows** the answer. (*know* is a state verb, so no continuous form)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 8 : Build the negative and the question
\begin{enonce}
For each affirmative sentence, write the negative form and the yes/no question.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item They have finished the test.
    \item He was sleeping at midnight.
    \item Sara works in a hospital.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Negative: They **have not finished** the test. — Question: **Have they finished** the test?
    \item Negative: He **was not sleeping** at midnight. — Question: **Was he sleeping** at midnight?
    \item Negative: Sara **does not work** in a hospital. — Question: **Does Sara work** in a hospital?
\end{enumerate}

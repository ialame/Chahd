### Exercice 1 : Choose the right modal
\begin{enonce}
Fill in each gap with the most suitable modal verb: can, can't, must, should, shouldn't.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item You ___ wear a seatbelt in the car; it is the law.
    \item She ___ speak three languages fluently.
    \item You ___ eat so much sugar; it is bad for your teeth.
    \item He ___ be at home, because all the lights are off.
    \item You look tired. You ___ go to bed early tonight.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Each modal expresses a different idea: obligation, ability, advice or deduction.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item You **must** wear a seatbelt in the car; it is the law. (obligation)
    \item She **can** speak three languages fluently. (ability)
    \item You **shouldn't** eat so much sugar; it is bad for your teeth. (advice)
    \item He **can't** be at home, because all the lights are off. (negative deduction)
    \item You look tired. You **should** go to bed early tonight. (advice)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 2 : Express obligation, prohibition and advice
\begin{enonce}
Rewrite each sentence using the modal that matches the idea in brackets.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item It is forbidden to smoke in the hospital. (prohibition)
    \item It is necessary to bring your identity card. (obligation)
    \item It is a good idea to revise your lessons every day. (advice)
    \item It is not necessary to pay; the entrance is free. (absence of obligation)
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Choose the modal according to the meaning: mustn't for prohibition, must for obligation, should for advice, don't have to for the absence of obligation.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item You **mustn't smoke** in the hospital.
    \item You **must bring** your identity card.
    \item You **should revise** your lessons every day.
    \item You **don't have to pay**; the entrance is free.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 3 : Ability and possibility
\begin{enonce}
Complete with the correct form of can or could (present, past or future ability/possibility).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item When I was a child, I ___ swim very well.
    \item I am sorry, I ___ help you now; I am too busy.
    \item With more practice, you ___ pass the exam next year.
    \item ___ you open the window, please?
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Can refers to the present, could to the past, and we use will be able to for the future.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item When I was a child, I **could** swim very well.
    \item I am sorry, I **can't** help you now; I am too busy.
    \item With more practice, you **will be able to** pass the exam next year.
    \item **Could** you open the window, please? (polite request)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 4 : Must vs have to / mustn't vs don't have to
\begin{enonce}
Choose the correct option and explain the difference in meaning.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Passengers (mustn't / don't have to) stand near the doors when the train moves.
    \item It is Sunday, so we (mustn't / don't have to) get up early.
    \item You (must / have to) wear a uniform at this school; it is a strict rule.
    \item I (mustn't / don't have to) finish this report today, but I prefer to.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Mustn't means it is forbidden; don't have to means it is not necessary.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Passengers **mustn't** stand near the doors. (it is dangerous and forbidden)
    \item We **don't have to** get up early. (it is not necessary)
    \item You **must** wear a uniform. (have to is also correct; both express obligation)
    \item I **don't have to** finish this report today. (no obligation, only a personal choice)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 5 : Logical deduction with must and can't
\begin{enonce}
Read the situation and complete with must or can't to make a logical deduction.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The ground is wet. It ___ have rained during the night.
    \item She never answers her phone at work. She ___ be very busy.
    \item This bag is so light. It ___ be full of books.
    \item He has been driving for ten hours. He ___ be exhausted.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
We use must for a deduction we are sure is true, and can't for a deduction we are sure is false.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item It **must** have rained during the night.
    \item She **must** be very busy.
    \item It **can't** be full of books. (a full bag would be heavy)
    \item He **must** be exhausted.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 6 : Past deduction
\begin{enonce}
Complete with must have, can't have or might have + past participle to draw a conclusion about the past.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The window is broken. Someone ___ (throw) a ball at it.
    \item He passed the test easily. He ___ (study) very hard. (certain)
    \item She isn't here yet. She ___ (miss) the bus, but I am not sure.
    \item The streets are empty and quiet. The match ___ (finish) already. (certain)
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Must have = sure it happened; can't have = sure it did not happen; might have = possible.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Someone **must have thrown** a ball at it.
    \item He **must have studied** very hard.
    \item She **might have missed** the bus.
    \item The match **must have finished** already.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 7 : Asking for and giving permission
\begin{enonce}
Rewrite each request or rule using can, could, may or be allowed to.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Is it permitted for me to use my phone here? (polite question)
    \item Students are permitted to leave the room after the bell.
    \item Visitors are not permitted to take photographs inside the museum.
    \item I want to ask, in a very polite way, to sit down.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
May and could are more formal and polite than can; not be allowed to expresses prohibition.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **May I** use my phone here? (or *Could I...*)
    \item Students **can** leave the room after the bell. (or *are allowed to leave*)
    \item Visitors **aren't allowed to** take photographs inside the museum.
    \item **Could I** sit down, please?
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 8 : Correct the mistake
\begin{enonce}
Each sentence contains one mistake with a modal verb. Correct it.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item You must to wear gloves in the laboratory.
    \item She cans drive a lorry.
    \item He must be tired yesterday after the long trip.
    \item You don't must touch the wires; it is dangerous.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A modal is followed by the bare infinitive (no *to*), never takes an *-s*, and for the past of deduction we use must have + past participle.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item You must **wear** gloves in the laboratory. (no *to* after *must*)
    \item She **can** drive a lorry. (no *-s* on a modal)
    \item He **must have been** tired yesterday after the long trip.
    \item You **mustn't** touch the wires; it is dangerous.
\end{enumerate}

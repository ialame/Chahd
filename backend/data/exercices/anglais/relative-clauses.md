### Exercice 1 : Combine with who, which or that
\begin{enonce}
Join each pair of sentences into one, using a relative pronoun (who, which or that).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I met a journalist. The journalist works for a local newspaper.
    \item This is the laptop. The laptop crashed during my exam.
    \item She gave me a book. The book changed my life.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Use **who** for people and **which/that** for things; the relative pronoun replaces the repeated noun.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I met a journalist **who** works for a local newspaper.
    \item This is the laptop **which** crashed during my exam. (or **that**)
    \item She gave me a book **that** changed my life. (or **which**)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 2 : Choose the right relative pronoun
\begin{enonce}
Fill in each gap with who, which, whose, where or when.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item That is the doctor ___ saved my brother's life.
    \item I will never forget the day ___ we won the trophy.
    \item Rabat is the city ___ my parents first met.
    \item He is the boy ___ father owns the bakery.
    \item The phone ___ I bought yesterday is already broken.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Choose the pronoun according to what it refers to: person, thing, possession, place or time.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item That is the doctor **who** saved my brother's life. (person, subject)
    \item I will never forget the day **when** we won the trophy. (time)
    \item Rabat is the city **where** my parents first met. (place)
    \item He is the boy **whose** father owns the bakery. (possession)
    \item The phone **which** I bought yesterday is already broken. (thing, object — *that* also works)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 3 : Whose for possession
\begin{enonce}
Combine the two sentences using whose.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I have a neighbour. His garden is full of roses.
    \item We helped a tourist. Her bag had been stolen.
    \item They live in a house. Its roof was damaged by the storm.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
**Whose** replaces *his/her/its/their* and shows that something belongs to the noun before it.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I have a neighbour **whose** garden is full of roses.
    \item We helped a tourist **whose** bag had been stolen.
    \item They live in a house **whose** roof was damaged by the storm.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 4 : Where and when
\begin{enonce}
Join the sentences using where or when.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item This is the school. I learned to read here.
    \item 2011 was the year. My family moved to the city then.
    \item Do you remember the café? We first talked there.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Use **where** for a place and **when** for a moment in time; both replace an expression like *here, there* or *then*.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item This is the school **where** I learned to read.
    \item 2011 was the year **when** my family moved to the city.
    \item Do you remember the café **where** we first talked?
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 5 : Defining or non-defining? Add the commas
\begin{enonce}
Add commas only where the relative clause is non-defining (extra, non-essential information).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item My uncle who lives in Canada is coming home next week. (I have several uncles.)
    \item Ahmed who is my best friend has just passed his exam.
    \item The students who failed the test must take it again.
    \item Mount Toubkal which is the highest peak in Morocco attracts many hikers.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
A **defining** clause is essential and takes no commas; a **non-defining** clause adds extra detail and is set off by commas.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item My uncle who lives in Canada is coming home next week. **(no commas — it tells us which uncle)**
    \item Ahmed**,** who is my best friend**,** has just passed his exam. **(non-defining — extra detail)**
    \item The students who failed the test must take it again. **(no commas — it defines which students)**
    \item Mount Toubkal**,** which is the highest peak in Morocco**,** attracts many hikers. **(non-defining)**
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 6 : Omit the relative pronoun
\begin{enonce}
In each sentence, decide whether the relative pronoun can be left out. If it can, rewrite the sentence without it.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The film that we watched last night was boring.
    \item The woman who lives next door is a nurse.
    \item This is the song which I love the most.
    \item The man who called you is waiting outside.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
The relative pronoun can be dropped only when it is the **object** of the clause, never when it is the **subject**.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The film **we watched** last night was boring. **(object — pronoun omitted)**
    \item The woman who lives next door is a nurse. **(subject — cannot be omitted)**
    \item This is the song **I love** the most. **(object — pronoun omitted)**
    \item The man who called you is waiting outside. **(subject — cannot be omitted)**
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 7 : Correct the mistake
\begin{enonce}
Each sentence has one mistake with the relative clause. Correct it.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item This is the teacher which helped me with my project.
    \item I know a girl who she speaks four languages.
    \item The village where I was born it is very quiet.
    \item She is the author whose books they are translated everywhere.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Use the right pronoun for people, and never repeat the subject of the relative clause.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item This is the teacher **who** helped me with my project. (*which* is for things)
    \item I know a girl who **speaks** four languages. (remove the extra *she*)
    \item The village where I was born **is** very quiet. (remove the extra *it*)
    \item She is the author whose books **are** translated everywhere. (remove the extra *they*)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 8 : Build the sentence
\begin{enonce}
Complete each sentence with a suitable relative clause of your own. A possible answer is given in the solution.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A dentist is a person ___ .
    \item A library is a place ___ .
    \item Friday is the day ___ .
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Any logical clause is accepted as long as the relative pronoun is correct. Possible answers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A dentist is a person **who takes care of our teeth**.
    \item A library is a place **where we can borrow books**.
    \item Friday is the day **when many people rest**.
\end{enumerate}

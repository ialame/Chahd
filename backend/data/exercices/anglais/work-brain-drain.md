### Exercice 1 : Gap-fill — the world of work
\begin{enonce}
Complete the sentences with the correct word from the list. There are two extra words.

*employment, unemployment, skills, qualifications, opportunity, expertise, training, salary*
\begin{enumerate}
    \item After finishing university, young people often struggle to find ___ that matches their studies.
    \item High ___ among graduates pushes many of them to look for jobs abroad.
    \item Modern companies look for candidates with strong digital ___ such as coding and data analysis.
    \item You usually need official ___ , like a diploma or a certificate, to apply for this position.
    \item Working abroad can be a real ___ to learn a new language and gain experience.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **employment**
    \item **unemployment**
    \item **skills**
    \item **qualifications**
    \item **opportunity**
\end{enumerate}
(The extra words were *expertise* and *training*.)

    \item **Unemployment**

    \item **qualifications**

### Exercice 3 : Match the word to its definition
\begin{enonce}
Match each word (1–6) with the correct definition (a–f).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item brain drain
    \item migration
    \item expertise
    \item developing country
    \item skills
    \item cooperation
\end{enumerate}
\begin{itemize}
    \item a. the movement of people from one place or country to another to live or work
    \item b. special knowledge or ability in a particular field
    \item c. a nation that is still building its economy and improving living standards
    \item d. the departure of highly educated people to richer countries
    \item e. the action of working together to reach a common goal
    \item f. the abilities you learn and use to do a task or a job well
\end{itemize}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item brain drain → **d**
    \item migration → **a**
    \item expertise → **b**
    \item developing country → **c**
    \item skills → **f**
    \item cooperation → **e**
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 6 : Opposites and word families
\begin{enonce}
Give the missing word in each pair, then complete the sentence with the correct member of the family.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item employment ≠ ___ . Many factories closed, so ___ increased.
    \item skilled ≠ ___ . An ___ worker earns less and is easily replaced.
    \item developed ≠ ___ . A ___ country often loses its best minds to richer ones.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item opposite: **unemployment** — Many factories closed, so **unemployment** increased.
    \item opposite: **unskilled** — An **unskilled** worker earns less and is easily replaced.
    \item opposite: **developing** — A **developing** country often loses its best minds to richer ones.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 8 : Spot and correct the wrong word
\begin{enonce}
Each sentence uses one word from our theme incorrectly. Replace it with a better word.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Our country needs to import more doctors abroad to its hospitals. (a word is missing or wrong)
    \item The unemployment of skilled engineers to Europe is a growing problem.
    \item She has all the unemployment needed for the job: a master's degree and five years of experience.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item *import* → **keep** (Our country needs to **keep** more doctors in its hospitals.)
    \item *unemployment* → **migration** (or **emigration**) of skilled engineers to Europe.
    \item *unemployment* → **qualifications** (She has all the **qualifications** needed for the job.)
\end{enumerate}

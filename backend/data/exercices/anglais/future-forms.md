### Exercice 1 : will or be going to?
\begin{enonce}
Complete each sentence with **will** or **be going to** (+ the verb in brackets). Choose according to the context.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Look at those dark clouds! It ___ (rain) in a few minutes.
    \item I've already decided: next year I ___ (study) engineering.
    \item The phone is ringing. Don't worry, I ___ (answer) it.
    \item Be careful with that ladder — you ___ (fall)!
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
Use **be going to** for plans already decided and for predictions based on present evidence; use **will** for instant decisions and simple offers.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item It **is going to rain** in a few minutes. (evidence: the clouds)
    \item Next year I **am going to study** engineering. (a decision already made)
    \item I **will answer** it. (an instant decision)
    \item You **are going to fall**! (evidence: the situation right now)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 2 : Present continuous for fixed arrangements
\begin{enonce}
Rewrite each sentence using the **present continuous** to talk about a fixed future arrangement. Keep the time expression.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item My plan is to meet the dentist tomorrow at four.
    \item Our class plans to visit the science museum on Friday.
    \item Her plan is to take the early train next Monday.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
The present continuous (**am/is/are + -ing**) is used for arrangements that are already organised, often with a person, a place and a time.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I **am meeting** the dentist tomorrow at four.
    \item Our class **is visiting** the science museum on Friday.
    \item She **is taking** the early train next Monday.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 3 : Present simple for timetables
\begin{enonce}
Put the verb in brackets into the **present simple** to talk about a fixed schedule or timetable.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The bus ___ (leave) at seven o'clock every morning.
    \item The film ___ (start) at eight tonight.
    \item The exam ___ (begin) at nine and ___ (finish) at noon.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
For timetables and programmes fixed by a schedule, English uses the **present simple**, even for the future.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The bus **leaves** at seven o'clock every morning.
    \item The film **starts** at eight tonight.
    \item The exam **begins** at nine and **finishes** at noon.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 4 : Gap-fill — choose the best future form
\begin{enonce}
Complete the text with the most natural future form (will, be going to, present continuous, or present simple).

*"Next week is busy. On Monday I ___ (1: meet) my study group at the library — we arranged it yesterday. I think the meeting ___ (2: be) useful. My train ___ (3: leave) at eight, so I must wake up early. Honestly, I ___ (4: not / waste) any more time before the exams!"*
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item **am meeting** (present continuous — a fixed arrangement)
    \item **will be** (will — an opinion or prediction with *I think*)
    \item **leaves** (present simple — a timetable)
    \item **am not going to waste** (be going to — a firm intention)
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 5 : Future continuous
\begin{enonce}
Put the verb into the **future continuous** (will be + -ing) to describe an action in progress at a future moment.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item This time tomorrow, I ___ (travel) to the coast.
    \item At nine tonight, she ___ (revise) for her history test.
    \item Don't call at six — we ___ (have) dinner then.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
The future continuous = **will be + -ing**. It shows that an action will already be in progress at a particular time in the future.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item This time tomorrow, I **will be travelling** to the coast.
    \item At nine tonight, she **will be revising** for her history test.
    \item We **will be having** dinner then.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 6 : Future perfect
\begin{enonce}
Put the verb into the **future perfect** (will have + past participle) to show an action completed before a future point.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item By the end of June, I ___ (finish) all my revision.
    \item By next year, they ___ (build) the new road.
    \item Call me at noon — by then I ___ (write) the whole essay.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
The future perfect = **will have + past participle**. It refers to an action that will be complete before a stated time, often with *by*.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item By the end of June, I **will have finished** all my revision.
    \item By next year, they **will have built** the new road.
    \item By then I **will have written** the whole essay.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 7 : Transform the meaning
\begin{enonce}
Rewrite each sentence using the future form in brackets, keeping the same meaning.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I intend to apply for a scholarship. (be going to)
    \item Perhaps the weather will improve tomorrow. (will / I think)
    \item My appointment with the teacher is arranged for Wednesday. (present continuous)
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item I **am going to apply** for a scholarship.
    \item I think the weather **will improve** tomorrow.
    \item I **am seeing** the teacher on Wednesday.
\end{enumerate}

### Exercice 8 : Correct the mistake
\begin{enonce}
Each sentence contains one mistake about future forms. Correct it.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Look out! You will drop those plates!
    \item I think it's going to be a sunny day, but I'm not sure.
    \item This time next week I will travel across the country.
    \item The train will leave at six, so don't be late.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enonce}

\textbf{Solution :}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Look out! You **are going to drop** those plates! (evidence right now, not *will*)
    \item I think it **will be** a sunny day, but I'm not sure. (an uncertain opinion takes *will*)
    \item This time next week I **will be travelling** across the country. (action in progress: future continuous)
    \item The train **leaves** at six, so don't be late. (a timetable: present simple)
\end{enumerate}
